# Assessment Objectives

1. Establish the control objectives and control requirements for portal authentication (MFA, password policy, account lockout, authentication monitoring, policy migration).
2. Collect and inventory the evidence available for each control.
3. Evaluate evidence quality — distinguishing what the evidence proves from what it merely suggests.
4. Assess **design effectiveness**: is the control designed to achieve its objective?
5. Assess **operating effectiveness**: did the control operate as designed during the assessment period?
6. Identify **control exceptions** and keep them distinct from evidence limitations and from actual security incidents.
7. Trace the exception to its **root cause**, separating root cause from contributing factors.
8. Assess **inherent risk** (threat × vulnerability, before controls) and **residual risk** (after existing and planned controls) for each risk arising from the findings.
9. Develop **risk treatments** proportionate to the assessed risk.
10. Define **measurable remediation acceptance criteria** so closure can be objectively validated.
11. Apply **sampling analysis** where population-wide conclusions are drawn, and state the limits of what a sample proves.
12. Map findings to **NIST CSF 2.0** outcomes, verifying every subcategory code against the official framework core.
13. Produce a **Business Impact Analysis** that separates potential impact from observed impact.
14. Deliver an **executive summary** a CISO or risk manager can act on without reading the workpapers.
15. Document all of the above as a coherent, reviewable workpaper set.
